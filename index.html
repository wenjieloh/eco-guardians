What Makes a Plant "Invasive"?

An invasive plant is a species that is non-native to an ecosystem and whose introduction causes harm to the environment, economy, or human health.


Not every foreign plant is invasive. A plant becomes truly invasive when it:

  • Establishes and reproduces successfully in a new environment
  • Spreads rapidly without human help
  • Outcompetes native species for sunlight, water, and nutrients
  • Disrupts native ecosystems and the animals that depend on them

In Singapore, invasive plants arrived via centuries of global trade, colonial-era ornamental planting, and the aquarium and gardening trades. Today, over 400 non-native plant species have been recorded here.

Singapore Example: Native vs Invasive

🌸 Tembusu Tree
NATIVE
  • On Singapore's $5 note
  • Supports 50+ animals
  • Evolved here over millennia
  • Has natural pest controls
  • Grows in natural balance
🌿 Zanzibar Yam
INVASIVE
  • Introduced from Africa
  • No local natural enemies
  • Smothers trees rapidly
  • Spreads via buried bulbils
  • Disrupts entire forest
🧬

Enemy Release Effect

In Africa, the Zanzibar Yam is controlled by insects and fungi. In Singapore, none of these exist — so it grows explosively.

🚢

Singapore's Port Problem

As the world's second-busiest port, Singapore receives cargo from 600+ ports. Seeds arrive constantly in soil, packaging, and produce.

🌡️

Climate Change Amplifier

Rising temperatures create even more favourable conditions for tropical invasives to establish and spread year-round.

How Invasives Spread in Singapore

Singapore's unique geography creates specific pathways for invasives:

🚢

Shipping & Trade

Singapore handles 37M+ containers/year — each a potential vector. Seeds hidden in soil, packaging, or produce.

🌊

Waterways & Drains

Singapore's canal network connects nature reserves to the coast. Water Hyacinth spreads rapidly through drainage systems.

🐦

Birds

Invasive Javan Mynas eat Senduduk Bulu berries and deposit seeds throughout nature reserves — an invasion meltdown.

🏡

Irresponsible Gardening

Aquarium plants dumped into canals and ornamentals thrown at forest edges are major problems in Singapore.

🏗️

Construction

Singapore's constant construction moves soil — and the seeds and rhizomes within it — to new locations where invasives thrive.

🌱

Bulbils & Rhizomes

The Zanzibar Yam drops bulbils that remain dormant in soil for years, sprouting when conditions are right.

Singapore's Most Threatened Habitats

Singapore has some of South-East Asia's most precious ecosystems — all under threat:

🌳

Bukit Timah Forest

One of the world's most biodiverse urban forests. Mile-a-Minute and Zanzibar Yam invade its edges, threatening species found nowhere else on Earth.

⚠️ Mile-a-Minute, Zanzibar Yam
🌊

Reservoirs & Waterways

Water Hyacinth mats reduce water quality, block sunlight, and clog pumping systems supplying Singapore's drinking water.

⚠️ Water Hyacinth, Salvinia
🌿

Sungei Buloh Mangroves

Critical nurseries for fish and resting spots for migratory birds. Invasive grasses and vines are fragmenting these coastal habitats.

⚠️ Casuarina, Para Grass
🏝️

Pulau Ubin

Singapore's biodiversity refuge. Invasives are filling gaps left by abandoned farmland — outcompeting returning native species.

⚠️ Siam Weed, Lantana
🌾

Wasteland & Grasslands

Open areas between urban and forest zones are invasion hotspots. Invasive grasses establish here first, then creep into adjacent forests.

⚠️ Siam Weed, Cogon Grass
🦅

Southern Ridges

This green corridor from Labrador to Mount Faber faces constant invasion from urban-adapted plants spreading from surrounding housing estates.

⚠️ Senduduk Bulu, Albizia

The Real Damage in Singapore 💀

The impact goes far beyond just looking untidy:

🌿 Ecological Damage

  • Biodiversity collapse — Invasives replace diverse communities with single-species monocultures
  • Food web disruption — Native insects, birds, and mammals depend on specific native plants
  • Soil chemistry changes — Some invasives alter soil pH, preventing native plant recovery
  • Light competition — Mile-a-Minute grows over forest canopy, starving trees of sunlight until they die
  • Long-term seed banks — Invasive bulbils and seeds persist for years, blocking native regeneration

💰 Economic Damage

  • Water supply costs — Water Hyacinth requires expensive treatment in reservoirs
  • PUB drainage blockage — Aquatic weeds block Singapore's canals, requiring costly maintenance
  • NParks removal budgets — Tens of millions annually on invasive management
  • Tourism impact — Degraded green spaces reduce eco-tourism appeal

🏥 Public Health

  • Dengue risk — Dense invasive mats create perfect Aedes mosquito breeding sites
  • Toxic plants — Some invasives in Singapore are toxic to children and pets
  • Water quality — Aquatic invasives enable bacteria and toxin-producing algae to bloom
  • Heat amplification — Invasives replacing native tree canopy worsen urban heat islands
🔦 Spotlight: What Mile-a-Minute Does to Bukit Timah

Growing up to 8cm per day, Mikania micrantha drapes over shrubs and trees like a thick green blanket, cutting off all light. Trees weaken and collapse. The seedbank fills with Mikania seeds. Native undergrowth vanishes entirely. Recovering a heavily invaded patch can take 10–20 years — or it may never recover.

How Singapore Fights Back 🛡️

Singapore uses integrated approaches combining government action with community involvement:

✋

Manual Removal

NParks Standard

NParks staff and volunteers physically uproot invasives. Must remove all roots — even small Zanzibar Yam fragments can regrow.

🧪

Targeted Herbicides

Specialist Use

Foliar sprays or stem injection. Used carefully to minimise impact on native plants. Not applied near waterways without special protocols.

🌱

Native Replanting

Critical Step

After removal, NParks replants native species to close the gap before invasives return. Volunteer planting events are vital!

🔬

Research & Monitoring

NUS + NParks

GPS mapping and citizen science data from iNaturalist. Early detection saves enormous costs in management.

📋

Biosecurity

AVA Regulated

AVA maintains a list of prohibited plants. All plant imports must be declared and inspected at Singapore's borders.

🏫

Education

Most Sustainable!

Long-term success depends on a public that understands invasive plants. That's why you learning this matters so much.

💡

Singapore tip: If you see Water Hyacinth in a canal, report to PUB's 24-hour hotline: 1800-284-6600. For other invasives, use NParks' feedback portal or call 1800-471-7300.

Singapore's Invasive Species 🔍

Eight invasive plants causing real damage right here. Click any card for the full profile.

Filter:
EXTREME

Zanzibar Yam

Dioscorea bulbifera

"The Bulb Bomber"

📍 Africa & Asia🌍 Island-wide📏 Up to 5m
🏞️ Habitat

Forest edges, secondary forests, roadsides across Singapore. Particularly aggressive at Bukit Timah, Central Catchment, and Pulau Ubin.

💀 How It Kills

Twines aggressively around native shrubs and trees, creating a dense canopy that blocks all sunlight — smothering whatever is underneath. Produces bulbils that drop to the ground and spread via soil disturbance.

📡 How It Spreads

Bulbils drop from the vine and are moved by water flow, soil disturbance during construction, and animals. Even small tuber fragments left in soil can regenerate a full plant.

🛡️ Removal
  • Manual uprooting — must remove all bulbils from soil
  • Repeated cutting over multiple seasons
  • Herbicide stem injection for established plants
  • Monitor for regrowth for at least 2 years
🤯 Wild fact: The Zanzibar Yam was likely introduced as a food crop during colonial times. Now it's one of Singapore's most problematic invasives.
EXTREME

Mile-a-Minute

Mikania micrantha

"Singapore's #1 Weed"

📍 C. & S. America🌍 Island-wide📏 8cm/day
🏞️ Habitat

Roadsides, forest edges, degraded forest, wasteland across Singapore. The single most widespread invasive plant in Singapore according to NParks.

💀 How It Kills

Grows up to 8cm per day, climbing over and smothering all vegetation beneath it. Forms a continuous canopy that blocks all light. Affected trees die within months.

📡 How It Spreads

Wind dispersal of lightweight seeds is primary. Seeds travel from construction sites and roadsides into adjacent forest. Stem fragments also root easily in Singapore's moist soil.

🛡️ Removal
  • Manual cutting before seed set — top priority
  • Repeated weekly cutting during growing season
  • Herbicide spraying on established infestations
  • Competitive planting of fast-growing native species
🤯 Wild fact: Introduced via contaminated grass seed in the 1960s — a single preventable mistake that created decades of ongoing management costs.
HIGH

Water Hyacinth

Eichhornia crassipes

"The Waterway Choker"

📍 S. America🌍 Canals, reservoirs📏 2x in 2 weeks
🏞️ Habitat

Singapore's reservoirs, canals, and slow-moving waterways. Common in Kranji Reservoir and various urban canals. Spreads rapidly after heavy rains.

💀 How It Kills

Thick floating mats block sunlight — fish suffocate as oxygen drops. Creates ideal breeding grounds for Aedes mosquitoes, increasing dengue risk.

📡 How It Spreads

Doubles coverage in 2 weeks through vegetative reproduction. Flood events wash mats through Singapore's canal network. Sometimes introduced when people dump aquarium plants.

🛡️ Removal
  • Mechanical harvesting on larger water bodies
  • Manual removal using nets and rakes in canals
  • Report to PUB: 1800-284-6600
  • NEVER dump aquarium plants into waterways
🤯 Wild fact: Introduced as an ornamental pond plant in the early 20th century because of its beautiful purple flowers — now one of Singapore's costliest aquatic invasives.
HIGH

Senduduk Bulu

Clidemia hirta

"The Forest Floor Killer"

📍 Tropical Americas🌍 Forest understorey🍇 8,000 seeds/plant
🏞️ Habitat

A serious understorey invader in Singapore's secondary forests including Bukit Timah and Central Catchment. Tolerates shade — can invade intact forest, not just disturbed edges.

💀 How It Kills

Forms dense thickets preventing native seedlings from establishing. Breaks the regeneration cycle of native forest — once it takes over the understorey, the forest cannot naturally replace its canopy trees.

📡 How It Spreads

Thousands of berries eaten by the invasive Javan Myna, dispersed throughout forest fragments. This bird-plant invasive partnership is called an "invasion meltdown" by scientists.

🛡️ Removal
  • Manual uprooting — most effective when young
  • Cut-stump herbicide for established shrubs
  • Long-term monitoring — seeds persist in soil
  • Replant native understorey species after clearing
🤯 Wild fact: Senduduk Bulu's success is partly powered by the Javan Myna — itself an invasive species — helping it spread. Two invasives helping each other!
HIGH

Siam Weed

Chromolaena odorata

"The Sunlight Thief"

📍 C. & S. America🌍 Wasteland, edges📏 3m/season
🏞️ Habitat

Roadsides, wasteland, forest edges, and abandoned farmland — especially prevalent on Pulau Ubin. Highly adaptable and drought-tolerant.

💀 How It Kills

Grows up to 3 metres tall in a single growing season, shading out all native plants below. Releases allelopathic chemicals inhibiting surrounding plants. Forms nearly impenetrable monocultures.

📡 How It Spreads

Wind-dispersed seeds in large quantities. Thrives especially in disturbed soil after construction — making Singapore's constant development a major driver.

🛡️ Removal
  • Cutting before seed set is critical
  • Herbicide on cut stumps
  • Replanting fast-growing native pioneer species
  • Volunteer cutting events on Pulau Ubin
🤯 Wild fact: Siam Weed is highly flammable when dry — a single discarded cigarette in a Siam Weed patch could start a fire devastating to Singapore's fire-free forests.
HIGH

Albizia

Falcataria moluccana

"The Widow-Maker"

📍 Maluku, Indonesia🌍 Parks, forests📏 7m/year
🏞️ Habitat

Extremely common across Singapore in secondary forests and parks. Deliberately planted for shade in the past — before its invasive and dangerous nature was understood.

💀 How It Kills

Up to 7m/year growth produces structurally weak wood that breaks catastrophically in storms. Dense canopy shades out native forest regeneration completely.

📡 How It Spreads

Wind and water-dispersed seeds in enormous quantities. Nitrogen-fixing roots give it an advantage in poor soils, outcompeting native trees.

🛡️ Removal
  • Ring-barking for large specimens — prevents sudden collapse
  • Controlled felling by arborists — dangerous due to structural weakness
  • Stump treatment with herbicide
  • Major ongoing NParks project on Pulau Ubin
🤯 Wild fact: Albizia was planted extensively for reforestation in the 1970s — a well-intentioned decision that created a major ecological problem Singapore is still managing today.
MEDIUM

Lantana

Lantana camara

"The Pretty Killer"

📍 C. America🌍 Gardens, edges⚠️ Toxic berries
🏞️ Habitat

Roadsides, garden escapes, forest edges, and Pulau Ubin wasteland. Still sold in Singapore nurseries as ornamental — still being actively spread by consumers.

💀 How It Kills

Dense prickly thickets exclude native plants and make areas impenetrable. Berries poisonous to children and most native mammals. Releases allelopathic chemicals suppressing surrounding plants.

📡 How It Spreads

Bird-dispersed seeds — particularly by Javan Myna and Asian Glossy Starling. Also escapes from gardens bordering nature areas.

🛡️ Removal
  • Manual removal with thick gloves (thorny)
  • Herbicide on cut stumps
  • Campaigns discouraging nursery purchases
  • 36+ biocontrol agents studied globally
🤯 Wild fact: Lantana flowers change colour as they age — yellow to orange to red — signalling to pollinators which flowers still have nectar. Beautiful but deadly invasive!
MEDIUM

Giant Salvinia

Salvinia molesta

"The Aquarium Escapee"

📍 S. America🌍 Ponds, canals📏 2x in 2.5 days
🏞️ Habitat

Ornamental ponds, slow-moving canals, and reservoir margins across Singapore. Frequently introduced through aquarium trade — people buy it then dump it in waterways.

💀 How It Kills

Doubles in 2.5 days. Creates thick mats blocking all light, depleting oxygen, and preventing gas exchange. Completely eliminates aquatic biodiversity beneath within weeks.

📡 How It Spreads

Almost entirely through human activity — aquarium plant releases are the main vector. Fragments travel through canals during heavy rain. Even tiny fragments become new plants.

🛡️ Removal
  • Manual skimming from ponds and canals
  • Biological control: Cyrtobagous salviniae weevil
  • Awareness campaigns targeting aquarium hobbyists
  • Regulation of aquatic plant sales
🤯 Wild fact: Giant Salvinia's water-repelling hairs are shaped like tiny egg-beaters. Scientists have studied them to develop new waterproof materials!
FIELD IDENTIFICATION

How to Spot an Invasive in Singapore 🔎

1

At a forest edge or disturbed area

Most Singapore invasives establish first in disturbed soil — construction edges, roadsides, gaps in vegetation.

2

Forming a monoculture

Healthy Singapore forest has extraordinary plant diversity. A large area dominated by just one species is suspicious.

3

Climbing or smothering other plants

Vines draping over shrubs and trees like a blanket — a classic sign of Zanzibar Yam or Mile-a-Minute.

4

Use iNaturalist Singapore

Photograph the plant and submit. The Singapore NParks community will help identify it within hours!

⚠️ Never remove Albizia yourself — it's structurally dangerous and can fall without warning. Always report to NParks: 1800-471-7300

HOW TO REPORT

Step-by-Step: Report an Invasive in Singapore

📱

Step 1: Download iNaturalist

Free on iOS and Android. Singapore has an active community — responses often come from NParks or NUS researchers within hours.

📸

Step 2: Photograph Clearly

Take multiple photos: whole plant, leaves (top and bottom), stem, flowers or fruits. More detail = more accurate identification.

📍

Step 3: Enable GPS Location

Make sure location services are on — precise GPS coordinates make citizen science data useful to researchers.

✅

Step 4: Submit the Observation

Upload to iNaturalist. Add notes about infestation size and density. All details help researchers prioritise management.

📢

Step 5: Report Formally if Needed

For serious infestations: NParks 1800-471-7300 or OneService app. For aquatic invasives: PUB 1800-284-6600.

SAFETY RULES

What NOT To Do ⚠️

🚫

Never remove Albizia yourself

Structurally dangerous. Can fall without warning. Only trained arborists should manage these trees.

🚫

Never dump plants in waterways

Illegal under the Environmental Protection and Management Act. One of the biggest ways invasives spread in Singapore.

🚫

Never pull and leave roots

Zanzibar Yam and Mile-a-Minute fragments left in soil will regrow. Bag all material as general waste — not green waste compost.

🚫

Never remove aquatic invasives alone

Deep canals and reservoirs are dangerous. Report to PUB and let specialist teams handle it.
